# BASC construction-start detection — negatives and the first between-class result, 2026-09-08

Follows `basc-construction-detection-2026-09-07.md`. That log closed with "NO performance
claim — the detector has never been run against negative campuses, so nothing here says it
can rank a queue." This one fetches the negatives and makes the claim.

**Status: negatives fetched, discrimination measured, 83 tests green. The detector
separates starting from non-starting campuses. NDVI reaches AUC 0.863 [0.714, 0.969] on
36 campuses. The lift number below is NOT comparable to production's queue bar — see §5.**

Repo: `github.com/danielcruz-dev/basc-construction-detection` (private, `main`).
Run log: `basc-negatives-2026-09-08.log`, `basc-series-2026-09-08.log`.

---

## 1. What was run

Two arms, sequentially, 4 workers, 3,204 CDSE calls, **0 failed**, ~19 min total.

| arm | campuses | AOI | rasters | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `neg20` | 20 negatives | Regrid parcel | 1,780 | the missing negative class |
| `pos16par` | the 16 sp16 positives | Regrid parcel | 1,424 | parcel-vs-parcel control |

Verified on the filesystem, not from the log line (2026-09-07 §4 mistake 5): 1,780 tifs in
20 dirs and 1,424 in 16, zero zero-byte files, zero stray `.part`.

**Negatives.** `stage ∈ {Announcement, Land Bank, Delayed}`, no verified start, has a
polygon — the pool `neg_sweep.py` already defined (seed 20260903). These are campuses whose
*recorded pipeline stage today* is pre-construction, so they had not started during the
window either. That is a stronger negative than "unlabelled".

**Matching.** 16 of the 20 are matched 1:1 to an sp16 positive on log parcel area and are
given **that positive's start period**. The remaining 4 close the area distribution and
enter the unpaired arms only.

    positive parcel ha   median 68.7
    negative parcel ha   median 72.9      ratio 1.06x, KS(log area) 0.100

The area match matters because 2026-09-07 §2 established that the AOI dominates everything;
an unmatched comparison would measure the AOI. The shared start period matters because
§5 of that log flagged February as a possible visibility artefact — an independent resample
put four negatives on 2025-06-H1 against one positive, which would have reintroduced exactly
that imbalance. Sharing the anchor cancels it.

**Pseudo-starts.** A negative has no start, so its window is anchored on a drawn one. That
is an anchor, not a label: it says where to centre the season-matched window and nothing
else. `grid.json` and the series carry `start_is_pseudo` and `class_label`, and
`basc_discriminate.py` refuses to run if a pseudo-started campus appears in the positive arm,
if an unflagged campus appears in the negative arm, or if any campus appears in both.

**One basis for everything.** All three series were read on `_endmembers_canonical.json`,
and `sp16` was **recomputed** onto it as `series_sp16_canon`. `series_sp16` had pooled its
own basis; comparing it against a differently-based parcel run would have repeated
2026-09-07 §4 mistake 1 verbatim.

The paired arm resamples PAIRS, not sites — the pair is the unit of replication once the
design is matched, and resampling the classes independently would discard the matching and
overstate the interval.

Leave-one-out, dropping each campus in turn: ndvi 0.853–0.896, high_albedo 0.832–0.892,
soil 0.727–0.794, vegetation 0.735–0.792. No single campus drives any of it.

15 of 16 positives and 17 of 20 negatives contribute. The dropped positive starts
2024-07-H2, whose two-year control lands in 2022, before the fetch span; the three dropped
negatives lose their onset window to cloud or snow.

## 3. The within-site test survives its first real placebo

The §3 result of 2026-09-07 was within-site: each campus against its own earlier seasons,
with a start-shifted-one-year placebo. That design cannot distinguish a construction
detector from a detector that fires on every campus every fortnight. The negatives are the
control it never had.

Running the **identical** `basc_test.py` on the 20 negatives at their pseudo-starts:

    metric        REAL mean            95% CI          verdict
    soil            -0.0040   [-0.0375, +0.0323]     no signal
    high_albedo     +0.0048   [-0.0018, +0.0139]     no signal
    vegetation      -0.0025   [-0.0281, +0.0242]     no signal
    low_albedo      +0.0017   [-0.0219, +0.0238]     no signal
    ndvi            -0.0090   [-0.0445, +0.0279]     no signal
    ndbi            -0.0110   [-0.0447, +0.0212]     no signal

**Every metric reads no signal on every negative.** The statistic that fires on 16 real
starts does not fire on 20 campuses that never started. The §3 result was not window
arithmetic.

## 4. Two findings that change what to build

**The parcel AOI is as good as the site plan.** Same 16 campuses, same basis, site-plan AOI
against Regrid parcel:

    metric        site-plan AOI    parcel AOI
    soil               +0.0808       +0.0809
    vegetation         -0.0538       -0.0546
    ndvi               -0.1476       -0.1422
    ndbi               +0.0383       +0.0431
    swir               +0.0289       +0.0264

Interchangeable. This does **not** refute 2026-09-07 §2 — the site-plan AOI on these 16 is
the union of *all* planned footprints buffered 100 m, effective median 54 ha against the
parcel's 68.7 ha, only a 1.3x difference. §2's finding compared a 320 ha parcel to a 400 m
box, a 20x difference. What it does establish is that **site plans are not on the critical
path**: §5 of the previous log recorded that only 12 of 229 verified-start sites resolve to
a site plan at all, and 11 of 27 have only sheets postdating their own start. The parcel is
enough for this statistic, and the parcel exists everywhere.

**The albedo endmembers are basis-unstable; NDVI is basis-free.** Recomputing sp16 on the
canonical basis reproduced §3 almost exactly for soil (+0.0808 vs +0.0814), vegetation
(-0.0538 vs -0.0548), ndvi (-0.1476 vs -0.1476), ndbi and swir (identical) — but both
albedo fractions **flipped sign**:

    high_albedo    -0.0604  (own basis)  ->  +0.0216  (canonical)
    low_albedo     +0.0337  (own basis)  ->  -0.0486  (canonical)

This is 2026-09-07 §6 item 7 confirmed with numbers: soil and vegetation have distinct
spectral shapes and stay anchored, while high and low albedo are the unanchored pair and
follow whichever basis they are projected onto. `low_albedo`'s inverted AUC (0.345) and
wrong-sign paired result are the same instability seen from the other side.

**So the headline metric should be NDVI, not high_albedo.** NDVI has the best AUC (0.863),
is computed from raw bands in `add_indices.py` and never touches the unmixing, so it carries
no basis dependence at all. `high_albedo`'s near-equal 0.843 rests on a quantity that
changed sign when the basis changed.

## 5. What this does NOT say — read before quoting a lift number

`basc_discriminate.py` reports precision and lift in the top 10: ndvi and high_albedo 1.71,
soil, vegetation and swir 1.49, ndbi 1.28, low_albedo 0.64.

**Do not compare that to production's lift ≤ 1.1x review-queue bar.** The base rate here is
0.444 (16 positives of 36) because the sample was built balanced and area-matched on
purpose. At that base rate a perfect top-10 ranking scores lift 2.25 — the ceiling, not the
detector's limit. A production queue has a far lower base rate and a far higher achievable
ceiling. **AUC is the transferable number; lift at 0.444 is not.**

Also still true:

* 36 campuses. The AUC intervals are wide and three lower bounds sit near 0.5.
* Negatives are drawn from a recorded pipeline stage. A campus recorded as "Announcement"
  that quietly broke ground is label noise on the negative side. That direction is
  conservative — it depresses measured separation — but it is not zero.
* This is a single fixed window per campus, not a chronological replay. No queue has been
  produced or scored, and nothing here measures time-to-detection.
* The 0.732 AUC baseline has not been run on this sample or this split, so §6 item 3 of the
  previous log is still open. 0.863 vs 0.732 is not a head-to-head.

## 8. Chronological replay — the 0.863 does not survive scanning

Added after §1-7, on the question of whether the detector can be pointed at the
932 pre-start campuses in the inventory. It cannot, on the strength of §2.

`basc_replay_causal.py`. Same seasonal comparison, made causal: at t the trailing
window is `[t-3 .. t]` against the same four calendar fortnights one and two years
earlier. Every term is at or before t. The §2/§3 window (`lo=-1, hi=+2`) reaches two
fortnights past the start and cannot be computed at t — the exact defect
`detect.py` describes for the old forward-looking persistence measure. `changed` is
`delta(t) > tau`, fed to the existing `run_causal` persistence rules. **tau is swept,
not chosen**; picking one on these 36 campuses would be fitting on the test set.

Nothing was ever built on a negative, so any confirmed call there is a false alarm.

    tau   detect  median   FA neg   per-window   campus
           rate     lag    campus    FA rate     WHETHER prec   lift
    0.06    0.12     +2     0.80      0.186          0.50       1.12
    0.08    0.25     +2     0.60      0.131          0.57       1.29
    0.10    0.25     +2     0.40      0.095          0.65       1.47
    0.14    0.31     +5     0.35      0.062          0.68       1.53
    0.18    0.19     +5     0.20      0.043          0.75       1.69

**Most starts are never found, and it is not latency.** Widening the hit window at
tau=0.14 saturates almost immediately:

    [start-2, start+2]   0.06      [start-2, start+9]    0.38
    [start-2, start+4]   0.12      [start-2, start+12]   0.38
    [start-2, start+6]   0.31      [start-2, start+24]   0.44

Given a **full year** after the recorded start, 56% of real starts are still never
confirmed. Relaxing `confirm_n` from 2 to 1 does not recover them either — it moves
detection at +6 from 0.31 to 0.31 while pushing the negative false-alarm rate from
0.35 to 0.45. The persistence rules are not the bottleneck; the per-period evidence is.

**It also fires long before the recorded start.** At tau=0.10, 8 of 16 positives
confirm more than two fortnights early, with a **median lead of 19 fortnights** and a
range of 3 to 45. That is 7-10 months, far too large to be §5's snowmelt-visibility
artefact, which would displace a date by a fortnight or two. Either the detector is
firing on something that is not construction, or site preparation genuinely precedes
the recorded start by most of a year. Unresolved, and it matters: those two readings
have opposite implications for the truth set.

**What this means for extrapolation.** The honest summary is that AUC 0.863 answers
"given the date, does a start look different" and the replay answers "can the date be
found", and only the second is what a screen over 932 announcements does. As a
*whether* screen the campus-level lift is 1.47-1.69 at tau 0.10-0.18 — above
production's 1.1x bar, but measured at this sample's balanced 0.44 base rate, not the
~5.3% implied by the 49 of 932 pre-start campuses that already carry a verified start.
As a *when* detector it is not usable: 31% detection at a +5 fortnight median lag.

Do not fetch the 932 on the strength of §2. The blocker is no longer negatives or
sample size — it is that the per-period evidence is too weak to survive scanning, and
that is a detector problem, not a data-volume problem.
