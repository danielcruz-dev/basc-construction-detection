================================================================================
CONSTRUCTION-START DETECTION -- STRUCTURE OF THE MODEL
BASC replication on Sentinel-2 / Aterio data-centre sites
Written 2026-09-07. Reflects the state of the work on that date.
================================================================================

This describes what the system computes, in what order, and why each step is
shaped the way it is. It is written for someone who has not seen the code. Where
a number appears it was measured in this repo, not assumed; where something is
unproven it says so.

Companion document: MODEL_CONTEXT.txt in ~/Aterio/acd-polygon-lab describes the
PREVIOUS generation (hand-written detectors on NDVI/NDBI/SWIR). This one
describes the replacement being evaluated.


================================================================================
0. WHAT THE SYSTEM IS FOR
================================================================================

Given a data-centre site and a fortnight, decide whether heavy construction
started there. "Started" means the visually obvious transition -- land cleared,
graded, foundations -- as confirmed by eye against satellite imagery.

The output is NOT an autonomous alarm. It fills a human review queue: each
fortnight a reviewer opens some number of sites and the system chooses which.
Success is "how many real starts land in a queue of K", not "accuracy".

Time is discretised into HALF-MONTHS ("periods"). 2024-03-H1 is 1-15 March,
2024-03-H2 is 16-31 March. One period is about 15 days. Offsets are in periods
relative to the verified start, so -4 means two months before.

THE NUMBER THAT GOVERNS EVERYTHING: the base rate is 3.6 real starts per
fortnight across a 932-site startable universe = 0.39%, roughly 1 in 260. A
detector firing on 20% of site-periods produces ~180 false alerts per real
start regardless of how good its "hit rate" looks.


================================================================================
1. PIPELINE, END TO END
================================================================================

    site + fortnight
        |
        v
    [1] AOI          which polygon on the ground do we measure?
        |
        v
    [2] FETCH        one Sentinel-2 raster per site per fortnight
        |            6 bands + cloud probability + validity mask
        v
    [3] MASK         SCL classes 4/5/6/7 AND s2cloudless CLP <= 65%
        |
        v
    [4] UNMIX        fully constrained LSMA -> 4 endmember fractions per PIXEL
        |
        v
    [5] REDUCE       mean fraction over the AOI, and AREA above a threshold
        |
        v
    [6] TEST         onset window vs the same season in earlier years,
                     with a placebo arm

Steps 1-5 are `basc_fetch.py` -> `basc_lsma.py` -> `basc_series.py`. Step 6 is
`basc_test.py`. `basc_chips.py` renders truecolor thumbnails from the same
arrays so the imagery and the numbers can be read side by side, and
`build_viz.py` assembles the viewer.


================================================================================
2. [1] THE AOI -- THE DECISION THAT MATTERS MOST
================================================================================

Everything downstream is an average over the AOI, so the AOI choice sets the
signal-to-noise before any modelling happens. Four definitions are implemented
and directly comparable in the viewer.

2.1 WHY THE REGRID PARCEL IS THE WRONG UNIT
Parcel areas run 4 ha to 1,864 ha (median 44.7, p90 155.5). A construction pad
is a few hectares, so on a large campus the event is a fraction of a percent of
the polygon and disappears into the mean. Concretely, 8,000 m2 as a share of:

    Microsoft WI, 320 ha parcel      0.25%
    Digital VA,   181 ha parcel      0.44%
    400 m box,     16 ha             5.00%
    1000 m box,   100 ha             0.80%

189 of 1,209 sites have no usable parcel at all (box_no_parcel /
box_parcel_rejected), and parcel SHAPE leaks into everything -- the QTS raster
is 370 px wide against Digital's 64, so even the imagery is not comparable.

2.2 WHY A BOX ON THE POINT IS NOT ENOUGH EITHER
Measured: take the per-pixel change across the start, keep the top 5%, and ask
how far those pixels sit from the site coordinate.

    site          bld   median   within 400m   within 1000m
    NTT IL          3    144 m       98%           100%
    Eagan MN        1     98 m       95%           100%
    Digital VA     11    375 m       26%            71%
    Microsoft WI    9    554 m        0%            39%
    QTS IA          7    751 m        0%             0%

All five coordinates are coord_confirmed. So on large multi-building campuses
the confirmed point is simply not where the construction is. Only 362 of 1,209
coordinates are confirmed at all.

A box forces a trade: capture the event, or be sensitive to it. Not both.

2.3 THE SITE-PLAN AOI
The monitor repo (aterio-data-centers-map-monitor, branch
feature/site-plans-batch-2026-09-07) carries
public/poc/site-plans/plan-meta.json: 105 georeferenced layers over 98
campuses, each with corner coordinates, per-building polygon RINGS, floor
areas, and a georeferencing fit block (residual_m, jackknife).

`--siteplan` takes the union of a campus's planned building footprints and
buffers it. This escapes the trade in 2.2 because it is not a box -- it is
shaped like the development, so it can span a 1.7 km campus while excluding the
surrounding farmland.

LEAKAGE GUARD, and it is not optional: a sheet dated AFTER the verified start
is refused. An AOI drawn around where the buildings ended up is not information
a detector could have had before they were built. This guard drops 11 of the 27
campuses that have both a verified start and a site plan, leaving 16.

(Judgement call worth revisiting: these are architectural drawings of PROPOSED
buildings, so a late-filed sheet may still represent pre-construction intent
rather than an as-built trace. The strict reading is the safe one and is what
runs; relaxing it would restore 27.)

BUFFERING IS DONE IN METRES, NOT DEGREES. A degree of longitude is
111.3*cos(lat) km against 110.5 km for latitude, so a raw degree buffer
stretches the AOI about 28% north-south at these latitudes. The geometry is
scaled by cos(lat), buffered, and scaled back.


================================================================================
3. [2][3] DATA AND MASKING
================================================================================

Source: Sentinel-2 L2A (surface reflectance) via the CDSE Process API, one
raster per site-period at native 10 m. Bands B02, B03, B04, B08, B11, B12 --
the six Wu & Murray need -- plus CLP and a validity mask, as float32 GeoTIFF.

WHY THE PROCESS API AND NOT THE STATISTICS API. The Statistics API returns
polygon AGGREGATES. LSMA, connected-component area rules and per-pixel change
all need PIXELS. The Process API costs the SAME ONE CALL per site-period and
returns the raster: a median 47 ha AOI at 10 m is about 68x68 px, roughly
110 KB in six float32 bands. Aggregation is a concession the Statistics API
forces, and it is what destroys the signal.

CLOUD IS TWO DIFFERENT PROBLEMS.

  (a) The tile-level `maxCloudCoverage` filter is measured over the whole
      ~110 km granule, not the AOI. Tightening it only creates gaps. Measured:
      relaxing it 40 -> 100 on one site recovered just 5 of 31 missing periods;
      22 came back with ZERO valid pixels even wide open. So it is left LOOSE
      at 100 and the real filtering is per pixel.

  (b) SCL alone misses thin cirrus, and thin cirrus unmixes as HIGH ALBEDO --
      precisely the fraction construction is supposed to move. Measured:
      high_albedo anti-correlates with valid-pixel count at -0.37 to -0.42, and
      one period SCL called entirely cloud-free carried CLP up to 185/255.

      Adding s2cloudless CLP <= 65% (0.65*255), as the paper does:

        period            px dropped   high_albedo SCL -> +CLP
        2025-04-H1 clear      0.6%        0.0241 -> 0.0238   (-0.1%)
        2025-06-H1 start      9.5%        0.0485 -> 0.0476   (-0.2%)
        2025-05-H1 cloudy     9.0%        0.0907 -> 0.0698   (-23%)
        2025-07-H2 cloudy     9.6%        0.1362 -> 0.0914   (-33%)

      Clean periods barely move; the spurious spikes lose a third. That is the
      right shape: it attacks the artefact and leaves the signal alone.

CLEAR_FRAC, NOT VALID_FRAC. valid_frac is measured against the raster's
BOUNDING BOX, so an irregular AOI can never reach 1.0 -- site-plan footprint
unions top out near 0.50, Regrid parcels near 0.72, ground squares at 1.00. One
cloud threshold across those confounds AOI SHAPE with cloud. So

    clear_frac = valid_frac / aoi_fill,     aoi_fill = poly.area / bbox.area

and clear_frac means the same thing for every AOI.

GAPS ARE REAL AND ARE NOT INTERPOLATED. Overall 69% of periods are clear. Of 76
gap runs, 51 are a single fortnight and 9 are 4+. Clear rate by month:

    01:35% 02:48% 03:60% 04:68% 05:82% 06:80%
    07:85% 08:72% 09:91% 10:100% 11:73% 12:43%

The long gaps are winter SNOW, not cloud. Interpolating across them is refused
on principle: filling a gap assumes nothing abrupt happened inside it, which is
exactly the hypothesis under test -- a real onset in the gap would be smoothed
into a ramp and misdated. Harmonic/CCDC/BFAST-family models consume irregular
observations natively and need no filling. The principled fix for cloud gaps is
SAR (Sentinel-1 sees through cloud), which remains unanswered.

A WORRY THIS RAISES ABOUT THE LABELS: verified starts concentrate in the
worst-observed months. February alone is 15.8% of all starts at 48% clear;
Jan-Mar carry 37.9% at 35-60%. Start-weighted clear rate 66% vs 70% unweighted.
If reviewers date a start to when disturbance BECAME VISIBLE after snowmelt
rather than when it began, that is a systematic bias in the ground truth and no
amount of cloud filtering would reveal it.


================================================================================
4. [4] LINEAR SPECTRAL MIXTURE ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Each pixel's reflectance is modelled as a mixture of four endmembers:

    VEGETATION       green vegetation
    SOIL             bare/exposed ground
    HIGH ALBEDO      bright impervious -- concrete, light roofs (and, honestly,
                     cloud and sand; Wu & Murray say so themselves)
    LOW ALBEDO       dark impervious, water, shadow

This is the Wu & Murray (2003) urban V-I-S set the paper adopts. Fully
constrained: fractions are non-negative and sum to one.

4.1 WHY FRACTIONS RATHER THAN INDICES
Construction is a BRIGHTNESS event: vegetation -> bare soil -> concrete.
Normalised ratio indices are DESIGNED to cancel overall brightness -- that is
their purpose, to remove illumination and topography. Using them here discards
the quantity that changes. Two consequences visible in the old model:

  * raw B11 ("swir"), the only NON-ratio feature, carries the largest
    coefficient in the whole logistic model at +0.82.
  * NDBI is structurally degenerate for this target. NDBI = (B11-B8)/(B11+B8),
    and bare soil AND finished buildings both have B11 > B8, so it cannot
    separate the two ends of the process being timed. Its measured weakness
    (0.023 paired AUC lift at one period) is not bad luck.

Fractions also carry a physical unit: fraction x AOI area = AREA IN SQUARE
METRES, which is what the 8,000 m2 minimum mapping unit is stated in.

4.2 ENDMEMBERS -- WHY NOT WU & MURRAY'S NUMBERS
The paper says it uses "the same endmember values introduced in Wu and Murray
(2003)". Those values are NOT PUBLISHED AS NUMBERS. Wu & Murray print them only
as a plot (their Fig. 5) and there is no table anywhere in the text; they were
image-derived from feature-space extremes of a single July-1999 Columbus, Ohio
ETM+ scene. Transplanting spectra from a 1999 Ohio scene onto 2025 Sentinel-2
over Iowa and Virginia would be indefensible even if the numbers existed.

So the METHOD is replicated, not the values:

    1. pool valid pixels across all sites in the run
    2. winsorise PER BAND at 0.5-1%
    3. PCA to 3 components
    4. N-FINDR: find the 4 pixels maximising simplex volume
    5. replace each vertex by the MEDIAN of its 200 nearest neighbours in PC
       space
    6. label by spectral character: greenest -> vegetation, brightest ->
       high albedo, darkest -> low albedo, remaining -> soil

Steps 2 and 5 are both necessary and both were added after seeing the failure.
N-FINDR maximises volume, so raw it chases whatever single pixel is most
extreme: it produced a "soil" endmember peaking at B12 = 0.85 (real soil peaks
at B11) and a "high albedo" rising into the NIR, which is an ice signature, not
a roof. A brightness-only trim does not catch the soil case because that vertex
sat at moderate mean brightness -- the trim must be per band.

4.3 ENDMEMBERS MUST BE SHARED ACROSS ANYTHING BEING COMPARED
Fractions are coordinates in a basis. Two runs with different endmembers are
not comparable, and a single small AOI does not span enough spectral range to
seat a simplex at all -- pooling from one site-plan AOI put "high albedo" at
brightness 0.18, DARKER than its own soil. `basc_series.py --endmembers`
reuses a fixed basis for exactly this reason.

This was caught late. An earlier reported result (Digital's high-albedo onset
delta rising from +0.022 to +0.034 under a tighter AOI) was an artefact of the
basis changing underneath the comparison; on a shared basis it is +0.022 ->
+0.018, slightly weaker.

4.4 SOLVING IT
Fully constrained least squares, exact and vectorised. For each of the 15
non-empty subsets of the 4 endmembers, the sum-to-one constrained LS problem
has a closed form whose matrix depends only on the endmembers, so it is
inverted once and applied to every pixel at once; the best feasible
(non-negative) subset wins. Verified to recover a known mixture to 4e-16.
A per-pixel NNLS loop would take hours on 3M pixels; this takes seconds.


================================================================================
5. [5] REDUCTION
================================================================================

Per AOI per period:

    <endmember>              mean fraction over valid pixels
    <endmember>_area_m2      pixels with fraction >= tau (0.5), times pixel area
    ndvi, ndbi, swir         the old feature set, from the SAME pixels and mask
    rmse                     mean unmixing residual -- a QA channel
    clear_frac, aoi_fill     see section 3

The area channel is the aggregate-safe stand-in for the paper's connected
components. It is NOT equivalent: 80 scattered bare pixels from tillage score
the same as one 8,000 m2 pad. Contiguity needs the pixels, which we now have --
implementing true connected components is open work.


================================================================================
6. [6] THE TEST
================================================================================

6.1 WHAT WAS WRONG WITH THE OBVIOUS TEST
Comparing an onset window (-1..+2) against a baseline window (-8..-3) compares
DIFFERENT SEASONS. In these data the seasonal swing is several times the
construction signal, so the comparison is dominated by green-up. Every early
result in this repo that used it should be read as noise.

6.2 THE DESIGN THAT WORKS
Compare the onset fortnights against THE SAME CALENDAR FORTNIGHTS one and two
years earlier, at the same site:

    delta = mean(onset) - mean(same fortnights, earlier years)

Each site is its own control, matched on season. This removes site geography
and the annual cycle together, and needs no separate negative-parcel sweep.

6.3 THE PLACEBO ARM
The same statistic is recomputed with the start shifted a whole year: same
season, no event. If the real statistic does not clearly beat the placebo, the
"signal" is window arithmetic -- the failure mode that produced v1/v4's 54-60%
"hit rates", which were an artefact of scoring P(>=1 flag in a 4-period window).

6.4 WHAT IT SHOWED ON FIVE SITES (n=5 -- machinery validation only)

    metric        REAL mean          95% CI      PLACEBO mean            verdict
    soil            +0.1126  [+0.0129,+0.2124]      -0.0314              REAL
    high_albedo     +0.0312  [+0.0152,+0.0472]      -0.0026              REAL
    vegetation      -0.1254  [-0.2175,-0.0352]      -0.0047              REAL
    ndvi            -0.2064  [-0.3346,-0.0782]      +0.0201              REAL
    ndbi            +0.0902  [-0.0065,+0.1869]      +0.0250              no signal
    swir            +0.0309  [-0.0073,+0.0628]      -0.0063              no signal
    low_albedo      -0.0184  [-0.0637,+0.0422]      +0.0387              no signal

    Two of the CURRENT detector's three inputs (ndbi, swir) fail to clear zero
    on data where soil and high_albedo clear it. A bootstrap over five points
    is not a confidence interval; this validates the code, not the claim.


================================================================================
7. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT HERE
================================================================================

CCDC. The paper runs CCDC and then filters its output with seven rules. CCDC
needs ~2 years of history to initialise AND a fitted POST-break segment to
produce Model Difference / Predicted Value After -- five of the seven rules
therefore cannot run in near-real-time, and a post-break segment is 7-8 months
of fortnights away. The paper's own success criterion is a detection within TWO
YEARS of the true date. This product needs a fortnight. Only 35 of 240 verified
starts currently have >=12 clear observations on both sides of the start
(recoverable to ~168 by fetching, about 2,500 calls).

CCDC would fix SEASONALITY, which the season-matched design in 6.2 also fixes,
more cheaply. It would NOT fix DILUTION, which is an AOI problem.

The paper's rule table is likewise not implemented: rules 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 all
need Cdif or Paft. What survives is the physical logic, not the thresholds --
and the thresholds are calibrated on Landsat (30 m, 8,000 m2 = 9 pixels) with
Landsat-derived endmembers on TOA reflectance, none of which transfer verbatim
to Sentinel-2 L2A at 10 m (8,000 m2 = 80 pixels).


================================================================================
8. WHAT IS ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT IS NOT
================================================================================

ESTABLISHED
  * The unmixing is correct (exact recovery to 4e-16) and the endmembers are
    physically sensible when pooled across enough sites.
  * CLP masking removes 23-33% of spurious high albedo on cloudy periods and
    <0.2% on clear ones.
  * The tile-level cloud filter is not a useful lever; per-pixel is.
  * AOI choice dominates: on large campuses the confirmed site point is not
    where the construction is, and the parcel mean cannot see an 8,000 m2 event.
  * Season-matched comparison finds structure that season-mismatched comparison
    does not.

NOT ESTABLISHED -- DO NOT CLAIM
  * That any of this detects construction better than the current model. There
    is no head-to-head against the 0.732 AUC baseline, and no negative class.
  * Anything from n=5.
  * That the site-plan AOI helps. On the one campus tested it gave the WEAKEST
    signal, and inside Digital Dulles' 7 planned footprints the high-albedo
    area never exceeds 0.09 ha across the whole 2023-2026 record, against an
    8,000 m2 (0.8 ha) bar -- i.e. nothing is ever built on those pads in this
    data. Either the works began elsewhere on the 181 ha campus or that start
    date is not supported by the imagery. That is a ground-truth question.

IN FLIGHT AT TIME OF WRITING
  * 16-campus site-plan run (1,424 rasters), fresh endmembers pooled across all
    16, then the section 6 test at n=16.
  * Note the 16 are not a random sample of the 240: campuses with municipal
    filings on file skew large and well-documented.

11. REVISION 2026-09-07b -- WHAT CHANGED AND WHY
================================================================================

11.1 LARGE PARCELS ARE TILED, NOT REJECTED
The 800 ha cap is gone. Size is a QUALITY signal now: the parcel is kept whole
as the search boundary, flagged needs_tiling above 100 ha, and searched by
overlapping tiles (tiling.py). Tiles overlap because a disturbance on a seam
would otherwise split into two sub-threshold fragments and be dropped by the
very filter meant to prevent that. Components are merged by SHARED GLOBAL
PIXELS via union-find, and the minimum-size filter is applied AFTER merging.
Merged disturbances are ranked by area, shape and proximity.

11.2 ROBUST AND RAW SCORES, BOTH REPORTED
seasonal_baseline(robust=) returns median/MAD or mean/sd; anomaly_scores()
returns both. The detector consumes the robust score; evaluation reports both,
so the estimator choice is auditable rather than asserted.

11.3 PERSISTENCE IS STRICTLY CAUSAL
The old forward-looking persistence could not be computed for the most recent
period -- the only period a queue cares about. detect.py walks observations in
order and emits, per period, from observations at or before it only:

    insufficient_evidence   too few clear looks. NOT the same as "no change":
                            a snowed-in site is not a quiet site.
    provisional_start       change present, not yet held long enough
    confirmed_start         held across confirm_n consecutive clear looks

Onset is dated to the FIRST sighting, not the confirming one. Cloud HOLDS a run
rather than breaking it -- an unobserved period is not counter-evidence. A
provisional call is a real output that upgrades in place when imagery arrives.

11.4 FOUNDATIONS NEED MULTIPLE EVIDENCE
15% is never called from brightness. Wu & Murray's high-albedo endmember
absorbs concrete, sand AND cloud, and this repo measured that fraction
anti-correlating with valid-pixel count at -0.37 to -0.42 -- it is partly a
cloud detector. 15% now requires foundation_min_evidence (default 4) of:
bright area, connected area, persistence, geometry, footprint proximity, prior
vegetation loss, visual confirmation -- over worked ground. With partial
evidence the model returns stage_group="10-15" and lower confidence rather than
guessing. Abstention is a supported output, not a failure.

11.5 GROUND TRUTH IS AN INTERVAL
truth.py brackets the start between the last confirmed-unchanged and the first
confirmed-changed observation. The REPORTED date is kept separately with its
own source and confidence and is never merged in. This matters because the
bracket is often wide: 69% clear overall, but 35% in January and 48% in
February -- the two months carrying 25.4% of all reported starts. Scoring to
the fortnight against a date that was never that precise manufactures both
false earliness and false lateness.

11.6 QUALITY SCORES REPLACE MOST HARD REJECTIONS
Only three things still reject a level outright: invalid geometry, unusable
georeferencing (residual > 60 m, ~6 pixels), and temporal leakage. Parcel size,
fragmentation, weak-but-usable georeferencing and point offset now deduct from
a quality score and raise warnings, itemised in rec["quality"]["deductions"].

11.7 SITE-PLAN METADATA IS RICHER, AND STRICT IS PRIMARY
siteplan_meta.PlanDoc separates document_date, revision_date, public_date,
plan_type and usable_from. usable_from is the LATEST known date, because a
detector cannot hold a document before it exists nor a filing before it is
filed. Two policies:
    strict  (default, primary evaluation) usable_from <= observation date
    relaxed (SENSITIVITY ONLY, labelled in every record via siteplan_policy)
            allows document_date, on the argument that these are drawings of
            proposed buildings
An as_built plan is never usable under either policy -- it records what was
built, so using it to find construction is circular. plan-meta.json currently
supplies only sheet_date, so public_date is unknown and every such record
carries "usable_from is optimistic" in date_provenance.

12. WHAT A VALID 0/5/10/15 DATASET STILL REQUIRES
================================================================================

None of the stage thresholds are fitted, because nothing here can fit them.
What is missing, in order of how much it blocks:

12.1 STAGE LABELS AT ALL. The 240 verified records carry a START DATE, not a
     stage. There is no example anywhere in this repo of a labelled "grading"
     or "foundation" observation. Every threshold in stage.py is therefore a
     legible guess. Needed: per site, per fortnight, a reviewed label in
     {0,5,10,15} with the interpreter's confidence and the imagery they used.

12.2 NEGATIVES. There is still no non-starting comparison set in this analysis.
     Without it, no precision, no false-alarm rate, and no lift statement is
     computable -- only within-site season-matched contrasts. The 150 parcels
     in results/negative_parcels.json are identified but have never been
     fetched at pixel level.

12.3 INTERVAL TRUTH, NOT DATES. Section 11.5 gives the structure; it needs
     populating from reviewed imagery, which means an interpreter recording
     the last-unchanged and first-changed observations rather than one date.

12.4 A RESOLUTION HONESTY CHECK. Whether 10% and 15% are separable at 10 m is
     an open empirical question, not an assumption. It needs a set of sites
     with high-resolution confirmation of pour dates, scored against what the
     Sentinel-2 features said. If they are not separable, the correct product
     is the 10-15 group and the four-stage ladder should collapse to three.

12.5 STRATIFICATION THAT MATCHES THE PORTFOLIO. The 16 site-plan campuses are
     not a random sample of the 240: campuses with municipal filings on file
     skew large and well documented. Any dataset built from them describes the
     detectable end of the portfolio. Stage labels must be sampled across AOI
     source (siteplan / parcel / point_box), parcel size, region and season --
     especially season, given the Jan-Feb clear-rate problem.

12.6 A DECISION ON THE RELAXED POLICY. Whether a plan filed after construction
     began still represents pre-construction intent is a judgement about the
     source documents that nobody in this repo is positioned to make. Until it
     is made, strict is primary and relaxed is reported separately.
